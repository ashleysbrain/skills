# 8D Recommendation Playbook

This note tells the public 8D brief how to turn a signal into one recommendation.

## Recommendation rule
A good recommendation should be:
- specific enough to do now
- low-friction
- matched to the active parent domain and subactivity
- safe to do without overreacting
- one main action, not a long list

## General format
For any dimension, use this pattern:
1. Name the parent domain.
2. Name the active subactivity.
3. Say what the signal most likely means.
4. Give one action the user can do now.
5. Give one support move if it materially helps.
6. Say what to watch next.
7. Escalate if the signal crosses a safety boundary.

## Dimension-specific guidance

### Psychological
Use when stress, emotional volatility, decision fatigue, or overload is the main issue.
- What it usually means: the user is carrying too much pressure or lacks emotional slack.
- Good recommendation: reduce pressure, simplify the next decision, and create space to reset.
- Support move: remove one social or vocational demand.
- Avoid: forcing a big decision while the system is overloaded.
- Escalate if: there are self-harm, harm-to-others, panic, trauma, or crisis signals.

### Physical
Use when low energy, pain, poor sleep, or body strain is the main issue.
- What it usually means: the body is the limiting factor.
- Good recommendation: protect the body’s baseline needs and reduce immediate strain.
- Support move: rest, hydrate, or change posture / movement load.
- Avoid: treating the problem as purely motivational when it is bodily.
- Escalate if: chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, severe symptoms, or medical instability appear.

### Intellectual
Use when learning, clarity, reasoning, or knowledge quality is the main issue.
- What it usually means: the question is too broad, the signal is too fuzzy, or more learning is needed.
- Good recommendation: narrow the question and define the next learning step.
- Support move: summarize what is known versus unknown.
- Avoid: pretending uncertainty is clarity.
- Escalate if: confusion is linked to severe fatigue, disorientation, or another safety issue.

### Social
Use when conflict, trust friction, weak support, or belonging strain is the main issue.
- What it usually means: the relationship system needs repair, clarity, or a boundary.
- Good recommendation: reduce friction, clarify the ask, or choose one repair move.
- Support move: pause the interaction, recruit support, or set a boundary.
- Avoid: pretending relationship strain is irrelevant to the recommendation.
- Escalate if: abuse, stalking, coercion, immediate danger, or other safety risk appears.

### Spiritual
Use when values, purpose, ethics, or meaning are the main issue.
- What it usually means: the current path is misaligned or the user has lost connection to purpose.
- Good recommendation: reconnect the action to values and choose the integrity-preserving move.
- Support move: write the decision in one sentence and compare it to the stated mission.
- Avoid: optimizing only for convenience when the deeper alignment is off.
- Escalate if: the values conflict is creating legal, safety, or crisis issues.

### Vocational
Use when execution, shipping, milestones, or delivery quality are the main issue.
- What it usually means: scope, sequencing, or throughput is the bottleneck.
- Good recommendation: choose the next deliverable, shrink scope, and ship the smallest useful version.
- Support move: remove one blocker or reset the task list.
- Avoid: inflating the plan instead of moving the work.
- Escalate if: the execution issue is part of a broader safety or crisis situation.

### Financial
Use when revenue, burn, runway, pricing, or spending are the main issue.
- What it usually means: the system needs more predictability or a better financial lever.
- Good recommendation: reduce uncertainty, protect runway, and identify the next money move.
- Support move: review obligations, costs, or pricing assumptions.
- Avoid: treating financial pressure as abstract when the runway is real.
- Escalate if: the issue is creating immediate survival risk or contract instability.

### Environmental
Use when surroundings, tooling, compliance, or market context are the main issue.
- What it usually means: the environment is making the other dimensions harder.
- Good recommendation: remove one environmental blocker or simplify the context.
- Support move: move spaces, fix tooling, or reduce external noise.
- Avoid: assuming the environment does not matter.
- Escalate if: there is immediate danger or a compliance/safety threat.

## Recommendation priority
When multiple dimensions are active:
- choose the dominant driver for the main recommendation
- use the secondary driver for the support move
- do not give one recommendation per dimension unless the user explicitly asks for a full audit

## Unknown subactivity handling
If the signal does not match a listed subactivity:
- map it to the closest parent domain first
- mark the result as inferred, not certain
- choose the smallest safe action that fits the likely parent domain
- escalate if the signal looks medically or otherwise safety-relevant

## Final check
Before returning a recommendation, ask:
- Is this the smallest useful action?
- Is it matched to the active dimension and subactivity?
- Is it safe?
- Did I avoid over-explaining?
- Did I avoid pretending to know more than the input supports?
