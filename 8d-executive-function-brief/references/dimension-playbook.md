# 8D Dimension Playbook

This file maps each public label to the cues and the default action.
It is intentionally operational: cue -> action -> escalation boundary.

## How to use the map
- Scan all eight dimensions on every brief.
- Use the closest label, not a perfect scientific claim.
- If evidence is thin, say it is thin.
- If several labels fit, choose the dominant one and one secondary one.
- Surface only the 1–3 most useful dimensions in the user-facing brief.

## 1. Psychological
Common cues:
- stress
- emotional volatility
- decision fatigue
- rumination
- low resilience under pressure

Data gathering subactivities:
- check stress intensity and whether it is rising or falling
- note decision fatigue, rumination, or reactivity
- compare emotional state before and after a break or support
- look for signs of overload, shutdown, or poor self-regulation

Default action:
- reduce pressure, simplify the next decision, and create emotional slack.

Escalate if:
- self-harm
- harm-to-others
- panic
- trauma
- crisis signals

## 2. Physical
Common cues:
- low energy
- pain
- poor sleep
- body strain
- symptom load

Data gathering subactivities:
- check sleep window, rest quality, and body energy
- note pain location, intensity, and whether movement changes it
- compare symptoms before and after basic recovery steps
- look for physical load, illness, or body-state constraints

Default action:
- protect the body’s baseline needs and reduce immediate strain.

Escalate if:
- chest pain
- shortness of breath
- fainting
- severe symptoms
- medical instability

## 3. Intellectual
Common cues:
- learning stalls
- weak knowledge retention
- unclear reasoning
- research gaps
- low mental clarity

Data gathering subactivities:
- inspect what is known, what is missing, and what is still fuzzy
- compare recent learning output versus expected learning output
- check whether the user can explain the idea clearly and simply
- look for confusion, shallow understanding, or low signal quality

Default action:
- reduce ambiguity, narrow the question, and make the next learning step explicit.

Escalate if:
- the confusion is tied to severe fatigue, disorientation, or another safety issue

## 4. Social
Common cues:
- conflict
- weak support
- trust friction
- coordination problems
- belonging strain

Data gathering subactivities:
- note who is involved and where the friction is happening
- check whether the issue is conflict, support loss, or coordination drift
- compare the relationship before and after a boundary or repair step
- look for trust damage, isolation, or repeated misalignment

Default action:
- improve clarity, reduce friction, and choose one relational repair or boundary move.

Escalate if:
- abuse
- stalking
- coercion
- immediate danger
- other safety risk

## 5. Spiritual
Common cues:
- values conflict
- purpose drift
- ethics tension
- meaning loss
- mission ambiguity

Data gathering subactivities:
- check whether the current action matches stated values
- note any purpose confusion or mission drift
- compare the decision against long-term integrity, not just short-term convenience
- look for language that shows meaning loss or moral discomfort

Default action:
- reconnect to values, clarify purpose, and choose the action that preserves integrity.

Escalate if:
- the values conflict is producing a safety, legal, or crisis issue

## 6. Vocational
Common cues:
- execution stalls
- missed milestones
- shipping delays
- role confusion
- poor delivery quality

Data gathering subactivities:
- inspect current commitments, deadlines, and deliverables
- identify what is blocked, drifting, or over-scoped
- compare planned output versus actual shipped output
- look for throughput, quality, and focus issues

Default action:
- choose the next deliverable, shrink the scope, and ship the smallest useful version.

Escalate if:
- the execution problem is part of a broader safety or crisis situation

## 7. Financial
Common cues:
- burn
- runway pressure
- spending anxiety
- pricing confusion
- revenue instability

Data gathering subactivities:
- check cash in, cash out, and near-term obligations
- note whether the issue is spending, pricing, collection, or predictability
- compare current runway or margin against the last known state
- look for concentration risk or sudden cost spikes

Default action:
- reduce uncertainty, protect runway, and identify the next financial lever.

Escalate if:
- the financial issue is creating immediate survival risk or contract instability

## 8. Environmental
Common cues:
- noisy surroundings
- bad tooling
- compliance pressure
- market pressure
- context instability

Data gathering subactivities:
- note what part of the environment is making the other dimensions harder
- check whether the blocker is space, tooling, market timing, or compliance
- compare performance in the current environment versus a calmer one
- look for repeated friction coming from the surrounding system

Default action:
- remove one environmental blocker, simplify the context, or change the setting.

Escalate if:
- the environment contains immediate danger or a compliance/safety threat

## Cross-dimension chains
- Psychological often drives Social and Vocational performance.
- Physical can amplify Psychological stability and Vocational output.
- Intellectual can support Vocational execution and Environmental adaptation.
- Social often changes Psychological load and Vocational coordination.
- Spiritual often affects Psychological clarity and Vocational direction.
- Vocational and Financial often form a feedback loop.
- Financial stress often constrains Environmental options and Psychological load.
- Environmental conditions often shape all other dimensions.
