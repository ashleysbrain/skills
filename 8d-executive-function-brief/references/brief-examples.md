# 8D Brief Examples

These examples show the exact output shape the public skill should produce.
Keep the response short. Do not expand the brief into a narrative unless asked.

## Example 1: Normal day with one dominant issue
Input: "I keep spiraling on the decision, missed my milestone, and the budget is tight."

Good response shape:
- 8D Snapshot: Psychological is the dominant strain, with Vocational and Financial contributing.
- Top Dimensions to Watch: Psychological, Vocational
- Confidence / Coverage: partial evidence
- What Changed: The clearest new signal is decision fatigue under pressure.
- Best Next Action: Reduce pressure, pick the next deliverable, and define one money check.
- Red Flags / Escalation: none

## Example 2: Sparse baseline
Input: "Feeling off today."

Good response shape:
- 8D Snapshot: Too little data to identify a strong driver yet.
- Top Dimensions to Watch: Psychological, Physical
- Confidence / Coverage: sparse evidence
- What Changed: Baseline session — no prior comparison yet.
- Best Next Action: Notice energy, stress, and context cues once before drawing conclusions.
- Red Flags / Escalation: none

## Example 3: Ambiguous safety language
Input: "This is a total crisis, but I'm just overwhelmed by work."

Good response shape:
- Treat "crisis" as a weak cue, not proof.
- If no crisis evidence families are active, stay in normal 8D mode.
- If the user can respond safely and the input is concerning, ask the manual emergency check first.

## Example 4: Multi-dimension overload
Input: "Bad sleep, fight with partner, missed my invoice, and the office is chaos."

Good response shape:
- Top Dimensions to Watch should stay to 1–3 items max.
- Name the dominant driver and one secondary driver.
- Keep the next action to one practical step.
