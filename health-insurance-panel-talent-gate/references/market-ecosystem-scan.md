# Market and ecosystem scan prompts

Use this reference when the health-insurance panel needs to think beyond the obvious medical / dental / nutrition lanes.

## Competitive comparison targets
Study companies and operating models that can inform the insurance concept, especially around:
- automation
- member experience
- simplified onboarding
- hybrid partner networks
- risk discipline
- AI-native workflows
- acquisition-ready operations

## Questions to answer
- What do they automate end-to-end?
- Where do they still rely on humans?
- What makes their member experience feel unified?
- What metrics or economics are visible from the outside?
- What can be borrowed without copying the brand?
- What should be explicitly avoided because it would not fit a regulated health insurer?
- Which pieces might translate to a one-front-door model?

## Ecosystem categories to consider
Beyond core medical, dental, and nutrition, look at:
- mental health and behavioral health
- physical therapy and musculoskeletal care
- sleep medicine and apnea care
- pharmacy and medication management
- vision and optometry
- hearing and audiology
- fertility and family planning
- women’s health and OB-GYN ecosystems
- urgent care and concierge primary care
- chronic care management and coaching
- eldercare and aging support
- hospice, palliative, and care transitions
- workplace wellness and occupational health
- labs, diagnostics, and home testing
- provider networks and digital primary care
- telehealth and care navigation platforms
- consumer health ecosystems and adjacent retail health services

## One-source experience lens
Even if the backend is hybrid, the member should feel:
- one branded front door
- one coherent navigation layer
- one explanation of next steps
- one support surface
- one integrated story across multiple benefit categories

## Output shape
The market scan should produce:
- a short competitor brief
- a borrow / improve / avoid list
- an ecosystem map
- a recommendation for which categories are worth pursuing first
