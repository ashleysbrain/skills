# Adaptive Personalization Science Notes

Session-specific synthesis for product-science hybrid work involving routines, scoring, confidence, and personalization.

## Core evidence themes

- **Habit formation is variable, not fixed.** A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis found median habit formation times around 59–66 days, mean times around 106–154 days, and wide individual variability (roughly 4–335 days). Frequency, timing, individual choice, affective judgement, behavioral regulation, and preparatory habits matter.
- **JITAIs/EMIs are appropriate for dynamic support.** Recent reviews/meta-analyses show small but real benefits for mental health and behavioral health, with stronger effects in some shorter, context-sensitive interventions. The field still needs clearer decision rules and more long-term evidence.
- **EMA/passive sensing are useful because symptoms and behavior fluctuate.** Momentary data can capture within-person variation and reveal links between state, context, and downstream behavior; adaptive support can be most useful when the user’s state shifts.
- **Personalized nutrition works modestly.** Recent meta-analysis suggests small improvements in weight/body fat, with inconsistent effects on many cardiometabolic markers and heterogeneous methods.
- **Tailored messaging helps, but not always more than active non-tailored support.** Tailoring is clearly better than passive control, but superiority over active alternatives is mixed.
- **Timing-based interventions are context-dependent.** For shift workers, time-restricted eating and similar timing interventions may be promising but current evidence is limited/inconclusive.

## Design implications

- Start with **population defaults**, then refine by **trait → context → state → response history**.
- Treat **sleep** as the day boundary for accumulation/sealing when the product is aligned to human physiology; use flexible anchor-sleep logic for shift workers.
- Keep **score**, **confidence**, **experience**, **quest**, and **goal** as distinct layers.
- Use **confidence** to represent certainty/coverage/corroboration, not gamified reward.
- Routines should be **modifiable in the short term** while also supporting **long-term scheduling** and habit building.
- If evidence is thin, **under-state** certainty rather than over-personalize early.

## Useful review questions

- Is the draft assuming one best routine for all users?
- Does it separate stable cohort rules from individualized adaptation?
- Are experiences, quests, and goals defined at different time scales?
- Is the confidence model about certainty rather than progress points?
- Does the system adapt to current state and sleep/wake timing?
- Does it preserve a path from mass-market defaults to deep personalization over time?

## Suggested references to check when refining claims

- Habit formation systematic review/meta-analysis (2024)
- JITAI mental health and behavioral health systematic reviews/meta-analyses (2025)
- EMA / passive sensing systematic reviews for health-state fluctuation capture
- Personalized nutrition systematic review/meta-analysis (2026)
- Tailored messaging systematic review (2026)
- Time-restricted eating in shift workers systematic review/meta-analysis (2025)
