# Full Paper Staging Pipeline — From Abstract to Conference to Marketing

How to stage a full conference paper AFTER abstract acceptance, including
staged writing, VERITAS gates, and marketing content derivatives. Developed
from the Physiomorphic AI / TSC 2026 San Diego pipeline (July 2026).

## When to Use

- Abstract accepted, full paper due in 8-12 weeks
- User wants the paper to serve double duty as a marketing engine
- User is establishing a new field or paradigm (field-naming paper)

## Paper Structure for a Field-Naming Paper (8-12 pages)

A field-naming paper has a specific architecture. It is not a standard
research paper. It must establish the field AND demonstrate the work.

### Section 1: The Ceiling (1.5 pages)

Name the existing paradigm that your field supersedes. Show its structural
limitation with citations. This is not a literature review — it is a
boundary argument.

**Example:** "Neuromorphic computing mimics the neuron. We argue this is a
structural ceiling: the neuron is one cell type in one organ system."

### Section 2: Defining the Field (1 page)

The defining sentence. The prior art on the term (competitive term sweep
results). The formal definition. The physics-informed principles.

**Critical:** This section cites the competitive landscape. If the word
exists in other domains, cite and distinguish them here.

### Section 3: The Method (2 pages)

The design methodology. How you map domain structure to computing
architecture. The isomorphism test: if the mapping is decorative (name
only, no functional correspondence), it fails.

Include a comparison table: your paradigm vs 4-5 closest neighbors across
5 dimensions.

### Section 4: Implementation (2-3 pages)

The working system. This is the proof the paradigm is buildable. Ground
in real architecture, real telemetry, real artifacts. No aspirational
claims.

### Section 5: Measurement (2 pages)

How you quantify the claims. Report ALL metrics. Where the system fails,
say so.

### Section 6: Discussion + Limitations (1 page)

The reframed question. What the field now needs. The honest limitations
(evidence level, no controlled experiments, etc.).

### Section 7: Conclusion (0.5 page)

The field is open. The implementation proves it is buildable. Future
work.

## Marketing Content Plan — The Paper as Engine

Each section generates downstream content. Plan the derivatives when you
plan the paper, not after.

| Paper Section | Marketing Asset | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract | Conference submission + press release | Portal + website |
| Section 1 (Ceiling) | "Why [old paradigm] isn't enough" | LinkedIn + blog |
| Section 2 (Defining) | The defining sentence as pinned post | X + LinkedIn |
| Section 3 (Method) | "Isomorphism vs Metaphor" long-form | Medium/Substack |
| Section 4 (Implementation) | Architecture diagram + organism map | Landing page |
| Section 5 (Measurement) | "How we measure [X]" infographic | LinkedIn + website |
| Full paper | arXiv preprint | arXiv (citation priority) |
| Conference talk | Video + slide deck | YouTube + website |

### Content to Build Immediately (before abstract submission)

1. **The defining sentence** in all author bios, conference profiles, social
2. **Domain registration** for the field name (e.g. physiomorphic.ai)
3. **Author bio update** to claim the field architect role

### Content After Abstract Acceptance

4. **LinkedIn announcement:** "I've coined [term] for a new paradigm..."
5. **Landing page** with abstract, defining sentence, competitive landscape,
   email signup for paper notification

### Content After Full Paper

6. **arXiv preprint** for citation priority
7. **Canonical diagram** (the one image that becomes the field's identity)
8. **Conference talk recording** for YouTube
