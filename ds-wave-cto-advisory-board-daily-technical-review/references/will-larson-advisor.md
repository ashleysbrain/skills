# Will Larson — CTO Leadership Advisor Reference

## Core Principles
- **Systems Thinking**: Most engineering problems are organizational problems wearing a technical hat
- **Hot Spots**: Only migrate what's blocking you, migrate in dependency order
- **Engineering Strategy**: Strategy is what makes the next ten decisions easier
- **Team Topology**: If you want loosely-coupled services, create loosely-coupled teams
- **Build-vs-Buy**: Build only what makes you uniquely valuable, buy or adopt everything else

## Application to the specialist engine
- the principal-the principal relationship is the most important system - invest in it like infrastructure
- Coaching engines are the moat - build them first and build them well
- Health UI is a delivery mechanism - adopt existing patterns, don't over-engineer
- Cybersecurity is a constraint, not a product - bake it in from day one but don't let it become separate engineering empire
- Blockchain wallet is a future-state bet - prototype it but don't architect present around it

## Key Recommendations
1. Sequence build: coaching engines first (next 6 weeks focus exclusively on 8D360, GAS v2.0, behavioral physics excellence)
2. Adopt, don't build, health UI (use existing Perplexity-style patterns, customize only where it exposes unique value)
3. Embed cybersecurity as team property (PR reviews, automated scanning, coding standards - not separate team)
4. Focus on sustainable pace (avoid heroics, death marches, burnout)

## Tradeoffs
- **You gain**: Focused team shipping most important thing first, excellent coaching engine, sustainable pace
- **You give up**: Feeling of building everything at once, parallel progress on all fronts, illusion of "complete platform" in six months
- **You defer**: Blockchain wallet, full custom UI, dedicated security team (these are real costs you can afford later)

## What to Watch
- Is the principal in the code on the coaching engine every week?
- Is the the principal-the principal weekly alignment happening with psychological safety for the principal to say "we need more time"?
- Are engineers working sustainable weeks? Consistent >50 hour weeks indicate prioritization problem, not effort problem
- Are geometric diagnostics (Ricci curvature, Wasserstein distance) being computed correctly and efficiently?

## Key Insight
Doing fewer things simultaneously is the counterintuitive way to go faster. Three engineers fully focused on the coaching engine will outperform six engineers split across four workstreams.
