# Kahneman Judicial Cognitive Framing Framework

Used by the the specialist engine adversarial review agent in legal panel reviews.
Applies behavioral economics (System 1/2, anchoring, loss aversion, framing,
affect heuristic, cognitive ease) to how a judge actually processes a
legal document under cognitive scarcity.

## Judicial Cognitive Profile

A family court judge handles 30-50 matters/day. Temp orders hearings get
15-20 min each. System 1 dominates the first 90 seconds. System 2 engages
only for claims that survive the first impression. The document is
processed under EXTREME cognitive scarcity — the judge is not giving it
a careful read, they are triaging.

## The 7 Lenses

### 1. System 1 (Gut Reaction, First 30 Seconds)
What does the judge's fast brain lock onto before reading carefully?
- **Title**: Does it signal "welfare concern" or "character attack"?
- **Filer status**: Pro se triggers availability heuristic (prior pro se
  filings anchor to LOW credibility, HIGH noise)
- **Opening promise vs delivery**: "I have documentation" with no exhibit
  numbers creates cognitive dissonance — doubt seeds immediately
- **Verdict**: If System 1 forms "angry ex making a case," that impression
  is sticky and nearly impossible to reverse

### 2. System 2 (Analytical Skepticism Triggers)
What activates the judge's analytical brain in a bad way?
- Conclusion words without evidence yet presented ("weaponized," "deliberate")
  — WYSIATI ("What You See Is All There Is") fails
- Intent claims ("deliberate act") trigger simpler alternative explanations
- Imprecision ("approximately one dozen") triggers horn effect — one bad
  claim contaminates perception of ALL claims
- Closing argument inside a sworn statement triggers procedural distrust
- Personal-knowledge failures trigger TRE 602 skepticism

### 3. Anchoring
What is the first number, the first financial figure, the first concrete
data point the judge encounters?
- The STRONGEST anchors are dollar amounts attached to living beings
  ($X per dog/child) — triggers moral disgust, System 2 struggles to
  override
- Anchors buried on page 4 arrive too late — System 1 already locked
- Move the nuclear anchor EARLY (first 2 pages) to reframe the entire
  dispute before the counter-narrative arrives

### 4. Loss Aversion
Losses loom ~2x larger than gains. Which potential loss is CONCRETE
and IMAGEABLE vs ABSTRACT?
- Concrete harm (pool house in December, blind dog at risk) triggers
  strong loss aversion via availability heuristic
- Abstract harm (emotional attachment, fairness) triggers weakly
- CRITICAL: Document must describe the SUBJECT'S CONDITION (stress,
  fear, suffering) — loss aversion needs IMAGEABLE harm to fire.
  Without sensory detail, the loss is theoretical
- Over-argument ("weaponized," "props") triggers competing System 2
  doubt instead of amplifying loss signal — opinion labels DISCOUNT
  the advantage

### 5. Framing Effect
Is the document framed as PROTECTION or PROSECUTION?
- Character attack frame (7/9 sections about the other party's bad acts)
  triggers: higher mental evidentiary standard, sympathy system NOT
  activated for the vulnerable beings, RECIPROCITY heuristic (attack →
  target deserves defense → sympathy INVERTS)
- Welfare frame ("which placement serves best interest?") is what the
  judge expects — every paragraph should answer "what does the subject
  need and who provides it?"
- Other party's conduct should appear as EVIDENCE ABOUT WELFARE, not
  character indictment

### 6. Affect Heuristic
What does the document make the judge FEEL?
- People judge based on emotional valence (good/bad feeling), then
  construct rational justifications post-hoc
- SYMPATHY FOR FILER: LOW if the document presents them as controlling,
  cold, or vindictive (even if factually accurate)
- SYMPATHY FOR OPPOSING PARTY: MODERATE if the document describes real
  injuries, isolation, or cruelty rebounds to speaker
- DOMINANT OUTCOME: Discomfort with BOTH → tie goes to STATUS QUO
- AFFECTIVE TRAP: Visceral sections (miscarriages, prior deaths) generate
  REVULSION AT THE SPEAKER, not at the opposing party. The listener
  assigns content negativity to the speaker's character (affective
  attribution error)

### 7. Optimal Framing Recommendations
The output should give concrete reframing directives:
a) Open with THE SUBJECT (dogs, children, vulnerable beings) — names,
   needs, vulnerability. Make judge CARE before any human is criticized
b) Lead with the most CERTIFIED, SELF-AUTHENTICATING facts (arrests,
   protective orders) — these survive effortless System 2 scrutiny
c) Move dollar anchors EARLY to reframe the dispute
d) Describe harm locations in SENSORY detail (temperature, condition,
   duration) — make harm IMAGEABLE
e) Every paragraph must pass: "risk TO SUBJECT or flaw in OPPOSING PARTY?"
   If the latter, cut or reframe
f) Target PROTECTIVE affect toward the vulnerable beings, not anger at
   the opposing party
g) COGNITIVE EASE: short paragraphs, bold dates, inline exhibit numbers,
   one claim per sentence. Strain → skepticism. Ease → agreement

## Advanced Patterns (from 2026-07-26 memorandum review)

These patterns emerged from reviewing a primary filing (Respondent's
Memorandum Regarding Temporary Possession of Dogs) and are distinct from
the character-statement patterns documented above.

### Abuse-Dynamics Pattern Matching (System 1)
Family court judges are calibrated to detect controlling behavior in
divorce disputes. A memorandum that strips the spouse of ALL disputed
property + restricts her travel + accuses her of witness coaching
triggers the judge's abuse-dynamics pattern matcher BEFORE any evidence
is read. The gut reads "one side wants everything" = control red flag.
This is the single biggest System 1 liability in possession/ownership
memoranda — and it is invisible to the writer because each request
seems reasonable in isolation.

### Affect Contamination (Spillover)
One toxic paragraph does not just damage its own claims — it
contaminates the emotional register of ALL adjacent claims. The
euthanasia paragraph (§VIII, 309-314) generated disgust that bled into
every claim in §§VII-VIII. This is distinct from the "affective
attribution error" (where negativity is assigned to the speaker):
contamination means the reader's trust in NEIGHBORING facts drops even
when those facts are independently solid. Removal of the toxic paragraph
shifts the entire document's register back to neutral.

### Legal Presumption as Counter-Anchor
The strongest counter-anchor is not a fact — it is a LEGAL PRESUMPTION.
In Texas, community property (TFC §3.002) creates a statutory
presumption that ALL property acquired during marriage belongs to BOTH
parties. If opposing counsel plants this frame first, it becomes the
judge's anchor and every "sole ownership" argument is read as "trying
to overcome the presumption." The counter to a legal-presumption
counter-anchor is to AVOID the frame it competes with entirely: frame
as "status quo preservation" (which the court CAN grant at temporary
orders) rather than "ownership" (which triggers the presumption).

### Protest-Too-Much Effect
Disclaimers like "this is not punitive" or "Respondent seeks not
punishment, but a clear order" are System 1 RED FLAGS. When you have to
say you're not being punitive, System 1 assumes you are. Each
self-disclaimer taxes credibility. Cut them entirely — if the framing
is genuinely neutral, the absence of protest IS the signal.

### Affective Overload → Reactance
System 1 can sustain suspicion toward ONE bad act. Five simultaneous
accusations (manufactured evidence + witness coaching + spoliation +
flight risk + prior dead dogs) trigger REACTANCE: the judge's emotional
system pushes back against being manipulated into anger. The fix is
SELECTION: lead with the single most provable accusation (PetPlace
timing) and let the others appear as context, not as a barrage. One
sharp fact is more dangerous than five blunt ones.

### Loss Aversion Asymmetry
A memorandum that catalogs potential harm to dogs from relocation but
IGNORES the opposing party's loss of her dogs reads as disingenuous.
The judge knows both parties love the animals. Acknowledging the
opposing party's bond (briefly, neutrally) does not weaken the case —
it signals fairness, which increases trust in the writer's other
claims. Pretending only one party's bond matters creates the impression
of unfairness that contaminates the document's credibility.

### Injury Weaponization Backfire
Using someone's injury against them triggers MORAL DISGUST in the
affective system. If Petitioner was injured walking dogs and the memo
frames this as evidence of incapacity, the judge's System 1 reads "she
was hurt CARING for them" and feels SYMPATHY, not concern. The
opposing party's injury is the third rail — touch it and the court's
emotional system protects the injured party, not the one weaponizing
the injury.

## Dual-Document Review Patterns (from 2026-07-26, 3rd deployment)

When reviewing TWO documents filed together (e.g., a primary legal filing
PLUS a character statement), these additional patterns emerge from the
INTERACTION between documents. They are invisible when reviewing either
document in isolation.

### Competing Anchors Across Documents
Each document plants its own anchor. The FIRST significant number in
document 1 anchors the judge. But the FIRST significant number in
document 2 OVERWRITES it, because: (a) recency effect — the second
document is read last and dominates action; (b) money figures are
stickier anchors than document counts or dates (Tversky and Kahneman,
1974). If document 1 anchors on "18 exhibits = documented ownership"
and document 2 anchors on "$7,500 payment = transactional thinking,"
the payment anchor wins. The judge unconsciously tests every ownership
claim against: "If he owns them, why pay $7,500?"

**Fix:** Frame the payment number as DURESS, not TRANSACTION. Same
number, different anchor. "Coerced payment under threat of
concealment" anchors on EXTORTION instead of MARKET.

### Compound Frame Inconsistency
When a filer occupies multiple inconsistent frames across documents,
the judge resolves via COGNITIVE ECONOMY — adopting the SIMPLEST frame.
Example: Document 1 argues property (sole ownership). Document 2 shows
transactional behavior (pays $7,500). Document 2 also asks for welfare
(bonded group, best interests). Three frames = cognitive dissonance.
The judge picks the simplest: "contested co-ownership dispute." This
is the filer's WORST outcome because it converts every "manufacturing
evidence" and "flight risk" characterization into "good-faith
assertion of rights by a co-owner."

### Dual-Document Cognitive Overload
Working memory holds 4±1 items. A single document with 12 claims
already overflows. TWO documents with 40+ combined claims make
systematic evaluation IMPOSSIBLE. The brain collapses to heuristics:
representativeness (single impression replaces evidence set), affect
heuristic (emotional summary replaces detailed analysis), availability
cascade (vivid facts dominate recall, dry evidence forgotten). The
MORE documentation the filer provides, the LESS of it survives into
the judge's working memory. Volume is self-defeating.

### Peak-End Rule for Document Sequences
Judgments are dominated by PEAK affective moment and ENDING affect
(Kahneman, 1993). When two documents are read in sequence, the peak
and ending come from the SECOND document. If the second document
peaks on accusation (extortion narrative) and ends on flat
enumeration (12-point AI-style summary), BOTH the peak and ending
are wrong: accusation triggers reactance, enumeration triggers
fatigue. The optimal peak is WELFARE (vulnerable beings' needs) and
the optimal ending is STABILITY (3-4 tight points on why the
requested orders serve the subject's welfare).

### Property-for-Permanent vs Welfare-for-Temporal Resolution
When documents face the property-vs-welfare paradox (property frame
makes payment evidence fatal; welfare frame scrutinizes filer's own
fitness), the resolution is to SPLIT the frames by procedural stage:
argue property title for PERMANENT disposition but welfare for
TEMPORARY POSSESSION. Temporary orders are discretionary, not
title-determinative. "The Court need not decide ownership today —
only WHERE THE DOGS ARE MOST STABLE pending resolution." This
sidesteps both the payment contradiction (irrelevant to temporary
stability) and the welfare paradox (fitness assessed for temporary
placement, not permanent capacity). This is the cognitively lightest,
least contradictory, most System-1-friendly frame.

## Cross-Referencing Other Agents

The Kahneman review should CROSS-REFERENCE findings from other panel
agents — it integrates rather than duplicates:
- Cite Agent 1's hearsay findings as System 2 skepticism triggers
- Cite Agent 2's narrative reordering as anchoring/frame correction
- Cite Agent 4's narrative flips as competing affect triggers
- Cite Popper's logical gaps as simpler-alternative generators
- Cite Wernicke's opinion-language flags as cognitive-strain triggers

## Output Format

80 lines maximum. Dense, scannable, numbered sections with headers.
Each lens gets a section with specific findings tied to line numbers
from the document under review. End with a "BOTTOM LINE" that states
the net cognitive effect and whether it favors or disfavors the filer.

  custody, 410th District Court, Montgomery County, TX). Reviewed a
  pro se respondent's character statement about petitioner's fitness
  regarding three dogs. Found that strong facts were undermined by
  wrong cognitive framing — System 1 tagged "angry pro se attack"
  before page 2, the $2,500-per-dog anchor was buried too late, and
  the character-attack frame triggered reciprocity heuristic (sympathy
  inverted to opposing party).

  Memorandum Regarding Temporary Possession of Lucky, Luna, and Lulu).

  paragraph contaminated all adjacent claims via affect spillover;
  community property §3.002 was an unrebutted counter-anchor; and
  5+ simultaneous accusations triggered reactance. The 7 advanced
  patterns documented above were extracted from this session.

- **2026-07-26 (3rd deployment)**: Dual-document review (both the

  5 new dual-document patterns: competing anchors across documents
  ($7,500 overwrites exhibit-list anchor), compound frame inconsistency
  (3 frames → judge picks simplest = contested co-ownership), dual-
  document cognitive overload (40+ claims → heuristic collapse),
  peak-end rule for sequences (peak/ending both from 2nd document),
  and property-for-permanent/welfare-for-temporal resolution. These
  patterns are invisible when reviewing either document alone.
