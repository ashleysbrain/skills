# Biohacking Marketing Advisor Selection

Use this reference when the user wants a the specialist engine or coaching-panel marketing seat for biohacking / quantified-self / longevity positioning.

## What the market is saying
- Biohacking and health optimization are framed around sleep, recovery, longevity, performance, and measurable self-improvement.
- The strongest messages are outcome-led, data-backed, and identity-specific.
- The market rewards founders who can speak to both the high-performance audience and the ordinary user who just wants something easy.
- AI-native positioning is strongest when it is framed as a practical execution layer, not just a novelty.

## Good advisor archetypes
- **Category founder / market creator**: useful when the user wants bold positioning and category framing.
- **Performance / longevity operator**: useful when the user wants audience trust in biohacking and human optimization.
- **Quantified-self / data-first voice**: useful when the user wants proof, metrics, and experimentation language.
- **Usability / adoption voice**: useful when the product must be simple enough for a non-technical family member to use.

## Selection heuristics
- If the product is health-first and performance-oriented, prefer a biohacking-native marketing voice.
- If the product is AI-first, keep the AI fleet or agent system in the story, but do not let it overwhelm the user-benefit message.
- If the product is aimed at both health/wellness buyers and tech buyers, the advisor should be able to translate between both worlds.
- Always preserve the simplicity constraint: powerful underneath, obvious on top.

## Practical examples
- **Biohacking growth seat**: Ben Greenfield-style lane.
- **Category creation seat**: Dave Asprey-style lane.
- **Quantified-self seat**: Bob Troia / Quantified Bob-style lane.

## Anti-patterns
- Using a generic growth advisor when the user asked for a niche market specialist.
- Selling the AI fleet as the whole story without tying it to a clear user outcome.
- Writing marketing copy that sounds advanced but would confuse a normal user.
- Treating simplicity as a “nice to have” instead of a positioning requirement.
