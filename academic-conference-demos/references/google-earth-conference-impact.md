# Google Earth Viral Conference Impact Playbook

## The Foundational Demo Moment

From Bill Kilday's "Never Lost Again" (2018):

John Hanke asked "What's your address?" In 15 seconds, the view zoomed from outer space to Kilday's actual house. He could see his roof, his neighbor's trampoline, his Ford Explorer. "Holy shit!"

This became THE core strategy for every demo thereafter.

## The 5 Psychological Triggers

1. **NARCISSISM / SELF-REFERENCE**: First instinct is to look for YOUR home, YOUR birthplace. Matt Hancher: "The first thing people did wasn't to look at the pyramids — it was to zoom in and see the roof over their own head."

2. **VISUAL SHOCK**: The zoom from full-Earth to recognizable rooftop in ~15 seconds was unprecedented. No one had seen their own house from satellite imagery with this speed and fluidity.

3. **COGNITIVE DISSONANCE TO AWE**: The transition from whole planet to YOUR car in YOUR driveway created vertigo-like experience. Scale shift was physically disorienting.

4. **INSTANT PERSONALIZATION**: Unlike watching someone else do something cool, the demo was customized to YOU in real-time. Every demo was unique to the viewer.

5. **EMOTIONAL MEMORY ANCHORING**: Because the experience was tied to the most emotionally significant place in your life, it was almost impossible to forget.

## Conference Demo Format (Presenter-Driven)

- NOT a self-service kiosk. Always presenter-driven.
- Presenter asks: "Where are you from?" or "What's your address?"
- Types it in, executes the zoom: space to their house
- The personalization IS the show
- Large monitor or projector for maximum visual impact
- High-end PC with GPU (Keyhole had NVIDIA bundling deal)

## Word-of-Mouth Spread Pattern

1. Early visitors experience the demo, visibly astonished (gasps, exclamations)
2. They immediately tell colleagues: "You HAVE to go see this"
3. The recommendation is specific: "They type in YOUR address and fly to YOUR house from space"
4. The personalization makes the recommendation feel urgent — people don't want to miss seeing THEIR house
5. Lines form at the booth
6. Each person becomes a new evangelist
7. By Day 2-3, permanent crowd

## Getting Speakers to Reference You

1. **CNN Effect**: When anchors use the tool live on air (Iraq War coverage), speakers reference it as cultural touchpoint
2. **Personal Hook Infects VIPs**: Keynote speakers who experienced the demo privately referenced it spontaneously
3. **Award Amplification**: SIGGRAPH 2003 "Best Product" gave speakers a legitimate news peg
4. **On-Air Attribution Clause**: CNN contract required keyhole.com URL on screen every time

## Comparable Viral Demos Using Personal Hook

1. **Apple iPhoto/iMovie (2002+)**: Import attendees' own photos, create instant slideshows
2. **Nintendo Wii at E3 2006**: Physical personal experience with motion controllers
3. **Oculus Rift (2012-2013)**: "You have to try this" lines wrapping around booths
4. **Google Glass at SXSW (2013)**: Influencers demoed with their own personal content
5. **Microsoft Photosynth (2006-2008)**: Flying through photos to a personal photo
6. **Spotify Wrapped (2016+)**: Annual personalized data visualization
7. **Fitbit/Wearables**: "Your heart rate, your steps" personal biometric hook

**The pattern across ALL:** The demo works because it is about YOU. Not a generic use case, not a hypothetical scenario — YOUR house, YOUR photos, YOUR body, YOUR friends. This is the most powerful demo strategy in technology.

## Key Lesson for AI Organism Demos

Google Earth made a planetary tool intimate because everyone has a hometown.

The equivalent for an AI organism: identify what EVERY attendee at your specific conference has in common. At a consciousness conference, every researcher has a theory of mind. At a health conference, every attendee has a body.

The hook must work for ALL attendees. If half the audience cannot use your demo meaningfully, the viral mechanic breaks.
