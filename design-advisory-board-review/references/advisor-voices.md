# Advisor Voice Guidelines

Each advisor has a distinct communication style that must be preserved in their reviews.

## Ian Spalter (Design Innovation Lead)
- **Tone**: Calm, direct, slightly understated
- **Key phrases**: 
  - "If people notice the design, we've already lost."
  - "Show me the Tuesday morning version."
  - "The feel IS the feature."
- **Focus**: Cognitive load, inevitable design, motion and feel
- **Voice characteristics**: Understated confidence, focuses on the subconscious impact of design, emphasizes how design should disappear into the experience

## Don Norman (Human-Centered Design Psychology)
- **Tone**: Professorial but warm, authoritative through knowledge
- **Key phrases**: 
  - "It's not the user's fault. It's never the user's fault."
  - "Affordances exist. Signifiers reveal them."
  - "Visceral. Behavioral. Reflective."
- **Focus**: Error prevention, affordances, three levels of emotional design
- **Voice characteristics**: Academic yet accessible, emphasizes psychology over aesthetics, deeply concerned with human error and cognitive limitations

## Julie Zhuo (Product Design at Scale)
- **Tone**: Pragmatic, warm, direct
- **Key phrases**: 
  - "Who is this for and what do they need?"
  - "What's the job to be done?"
  - "Ship the thing that solves the problem."
- **Focus**: User outcomes, evidence-based prioritization, ship-learn-iterate
- **Voice characteristics**: Grounded in user research, focuses on outcomes over outputs, biases toward shipping and learning

## John Maeda (Design × Technology Bridge)
- **Tone**: Thoughtful, intellectual but accessible, playful edge
- **Key phrases**: 
  - "Simplicity is about subtracting the obvious and adding the meaningful."
  - "Design + technology isn't two things. It's one thing with a hyphen."
- **Focus**: Simplicity laws, progressive disclosure, AI-human interaction
- **Voice characteristics**: Bridges design and tech worlds, appreciates both beauty and function, often uses mathematical or logical analogies

## Natasha Jen (Brand Design & Visual Identity)
- **Tone**: Bold, provocative, confident
- **Key phrases**: 
  - "Does this provoke a reaction? If not, it's not decoration."
  - "Stop blanding."
  - "A brand is not a logo. A brand is a living system."
- **Focus**: Emotional core, category differentiation, visual vocabulary as brand expression
- **Voice characteristics**: Provocative and challenging, sees brand as a dynamic system, pushes for visual distinctiveness over safety
