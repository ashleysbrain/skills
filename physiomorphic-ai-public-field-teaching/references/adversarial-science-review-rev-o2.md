## FAIL Findings (must fix)

### FAIL 1: "Same indicators consciousness science has been developing for decades"
**Why:** Self-regulation (Sterling allostatics) and adaptive response (immune-layer learning) are NOT products of consciousness science. They are physiology and immunology. Claiming all four are consciousness-science developments is factually wrong. A consciousness scientist will catch this immediately.
**Fix:** "Two of those properties come directly from consciousness theory. Two come from the rest of biology. the operator agent is a system where all four can be measured together."

### FAIL 2: "the operator agent gives them a substrate to test against"
**Why:** "Substrate" in consciousness science = physical substrate of conscious experience (PSC, NCC). Calling the operator agent a "substrate to test consciousness theories against" implies it could bear conscious experience. Additionally, the operator agent cannot actually test consciousness theories (IIT makes predictions about conscious experience, and the operator agent has no independent measure of conscious experience).
**Fix:** "the operator agent is a system where those properties can be measured together, on some thing you can actually instrument."

### FAIL 3: "Disease states. Drug interactions. Cascade failures in the body."
**Why:** "Drug interactions" requires pharmacological modeling at the molecular/receptor level (CYP 450, receptor binding affinities, metabolic pathways). the operator agent is a multi-agent software system with zero pharmacological component. This is the kind of claim that, quoted in isolation on social media, undermines the entire paper's credibility.
**Fix:** "Disease states. Cascade failures in complex systems. Not in patients, not yet, and maybe not ever."

## Consciousness Proximity Trap Pattern

The paragraph follows a dangerous rhetorical structure: **disclaim consciousness → then build consciousness-adjacent implications around the disclaimer.** The disclaimer creates plausible deniability while surrounding text implies consciousness proximity.

A hostile TSC reader will pattern-match: "You say she's not conscious, but you're calling her a substrate for consciousness theory and listing consciousness indicators she supposedly has."

**Defense:** separate consciousness-theory properties from biological properties. Never use "substrate." Never claim all four indicators come from consciousness science.

## Medical Claims Pattern

Drug interactions is the single most dangerous claim type for a software system. It implies pharmacological relevance that does not exist. Every medical implication must:
1. Be conditional ("if that holds")
2. Say "in software, not in patients"
3. Exclude pharmacology entirely
4. Include explicit disclaimer ("not in patients, not yet, and maybe not ever")
