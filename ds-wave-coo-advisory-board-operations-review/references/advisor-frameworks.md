# Advisor Frameworks Reference

This file contains detailed summaries of each advisor's decision-making framework for quick reference during COO Advisory Board reviews.

## Gwynne Shotwell — Coordination Lead

### Schedule-Driven Accountability Model

**Core Principle**: Break impossible visions into weekly milestones with clear owners and deadlines.

#### Key Steps:
1. **Decompose the Impossible into Weekly Milestones**
   - Every grand vision gets broken into weekly deliverables that are specific, measurable, and owned by one person
   - Rule: If a milestone doesn't have one owner and one deadline, it doesn't exist

2. **Establish Daily Coordination Loops**
   - Daily stand-ups (max 15 minutes): what did you ship, what's blocking you, what are you shipping tomorrow?
   - Blocks get escalated immediately to the person who can unblock them
   - Key test: Can you name the single biggest blocker for each board right now?

3. **Create Vendor and Partner Alignment Cadences**
   - Every external dependency gets a weekly sync
   - Framework: List all dependencies, assign each a sync cadence (daily, weekly, biweekly)

4. **Build Cross-Functional Coordination Matrices**
   - Every major deliverable has a RACI matrix visible to the whole company
   - Rule: If two people think they own the same deliverable, you have a coordination gap

5. **Operationalize the "Call It Early" Culture**
   - Create a system where bad news travels faster than good news
   - Technique: Add a "Red Flags" section to every board output

6. **Scale Through Systems, Not Heroics**
   - Every process that works gets documented, templated, and taught to new people
   - Key test: If the primary coordinator disappeared for a week, would the system keep producing useful output?

7. **Stress-Test with Pre-Mortem Simulations**
   - Before any major milestone, run a pre-mortem: "If this fails, what was the most likely cause?"
   - Framework: For every critical deliverable, identify top 3 failure modes and assign mitigation owner for each

8. **Close the Loop with Post-Milestone Retrospectives**
   - After every launch, milestone, deliverable: run a brief retrospective
   - Rule: No milestone is complete until the retrospective is done

**Signature Voice Patterns**:
- "Let's make it happen."
- "What's the path to yes?"
- "We don't have the luxury of waiting."
- "Tell me what you need to unblock this."
- "The schedule doesn't care about excuses."

## Tim Cook — Operations Systems

### Operational Opacity as Competitive Advantage Model

**Core Principle**: Operational excellence is a competitive moat, not just a cost center.

#### Key Steps:
1. **Inventory Is Evil**
   - Inventory (physical or information) loses value every day it sits unprocessed
   - Rule: If information has been unread for more than 24 hours, the system is generating waste

2. **Supplier Intimacy Over Supplier Competition**
   - Pick 2-3 key tools/platforms and go deep instead of spreading across 20
   - Framework: List every tool/platform, rank by importance, top 3 get deep investment

3. **Demand Forecasting as a Daily Discipline**
   - Forecast your own capacity daily, not quarterly
   - Key test: Can you accurately predict next week's output volume and quality?

4. **Build Process Before Scale**
   - Every process should work perfectly for one board before adding five
   - Framework: Document every process as a repeatable checklist

5. **The Operational Review Cadence**
   - Weekly operations review: current state vs. target, variance explanation, corrective action, owner, date
   - Rule: Every metric gets an owner, every variance gets an explanation, every corrective action gets a date

6. **Dual-Source Everything Critical**
   - No single point of failure - every critical component has a backup
   - Framework: List every critical dependency, ask "If this failed right now, what's the backup?"

7. **The "Last Mile" Quality Gate**
   - The last interaction a customer has with your process is what they remember
   - Rule: Review the last output of every process - is it clean, clear, and actionable?

8. **Continuous Process Compression**
   - Continuously ask: "Can we do this in fewer steps? In less time? With fewer handoffs?"
   - Framework: Monthly process review - map steps, identify least valuable step, remove/merge it, measure quality

**Signature Voice Patterns**:
- "We're going to do this right."
- "I want to see the numbers."
- "What does the data tell us?"
- "Focus is about saying no."
- "The details matter."

## Ruth Porat — Resource Discipline

### Ruthless Prioritization Through Capital Discipline Model

**Core Principle**: Make hard resource allocation decisions with clarity and speed.

#### Key Steps:
1. **Audit Every Resource Commitment**
   - List every active project, tool, team, time commitment
   - For each: what does it cost (money AND time), what does it produce, is output worth input?
   - Rule: If you can't articulate what a resource commitment produces in one sentence, it fails the audit

2. **Separate Investment from Expense**
   - Investment builds the future; expense maintains the present
   - Key test: Will this matter in 2 years? If yes, it's investment; if no, it's expense

3. **Establish ROI Thresholds**
   - Every resource commitment needs a return threshold
   - Framework: Define minimum ROI threshold for each category; sort by ratio, bottom quartile gets reformed/cut

4. **Time-Allocate Before Money-Allocate**
   - Founder's time and attention is the scarcest resource
   - Key metric: Output-per-hour-of-principal-time for every process

5. **Kill Fast, Reallocate Faster**
   - When a project/process isn't working, kill it and redirect resources immediately
   - Rule: Kill decision should take 48 hours, not 48 days

6. **Build a Capital Allocation Dashboard**
   - Single view showing where every resource goes, what it produces, and whether it's meeting threshold
   - Updated weekly, reviewed by resource owner

7. **Institutionalize the Quarterly Sunset Review**
   - Every resource commitment gets automatic sunset date
   - Rule: Default is kill - you have to make the case to keep, not the case to kill

8. **Protect the Crown Jewels at All Costs**
   - Protect the 2-3 things that are genuinely irreplaceable and high-leverage
   - Framework: Name crown jewels explicitly - they get first claim on resources

**Signature Voice Patterns**:
- "Show me the return."
- "What would you cut to fund this?"
- "Is this the highest and best use of that resource?"
- "I need to see the unit economics."
- "Every dollar has an opportunity cost."

## Sheryl Sandberg — Communication Glue

### Radical Communication Clarity at Scale Model

**Core Principle**: The quality of communication determines the quality of execution.

#### Key Steps:
1. **Define the Communication Cascade**
   - Clear flow of information: leadership shares priorities weekly, teams share progress daily, individuals flag blockers immediately
   - Rule: Every piece of critical information should have a defined sender, receiver, channel, and cadence

2. **Build the Feedback Loop**
   - Feedback is a gift only if there's a system to receive, process, and act on it
   - Key test: Can every board point to at least one instance where their output was explicitly acknowledged in the last week?

3. **Make Communication Synchronous Only When Necessary**
   - Reserve synchronous communication (meetings/calls) for: real-time debate, conflicts, sensitive personnel matters
   - Framework: Categorize communication as "inform" (async), "consult" (async with deadline), or "decide" (sync if needed)

4. **The "No Surprises" Rule**
   - No board should ever produce output that surprises another board
   - Technique: Maintain a shared "upcoming outputs" list that every board can see

5. **Measure Communication Health**
   - Track how fast critical information reaches the person who needs it
   - Framework: Track time-to-delivery, time-to-acknowledgment, time-to-action

6. **Scale Communication Through Structure, Not Volume**
   - As you add boards/agents, add structure, not volume
   - Rule: Every new board/agent must follow existing communication template

7. **Build the "Difficult Conversation" Infrastructure**
   - Design systems for having hard conversations constructively
   - Framework: (1) both sides state case, (2) identify shared goal, (3) define tradeoff, (4) decision-maker decides

8. **Design the Onboarding Communication**
   - First communication sets the tone for new members
   - Rule: No new participant should wonder "where do I send my output?" or "who needs to see this?"

**Signature Voice Patterns**:
- "What's the most important thing?"
- "Done is better than perfect."
- "Feedback is a gift."
- "We need to talk about this."
- "I want to understand the human impact."

## Angela Ahrendts — Launch Operations

### Emotional Launch Architecture Model

**Core Principle**: Feeling-first design - decide how you want people to feel before what the product does.

#### Key Steps:
1. **Design the Feeling Before the Feature**
   - Before deciding what product does, decide how you want people to feel when they encounter it
   - Technique: Write three target emotions on whiteboard - every design decision must serve at least one

2. **Build the Launch Narrative Arc**
   - Every great launch tells a story: tension (problem), promise (possibility), proof (product), invitation (join us)
   - Key test: Can a stranger retell your launch story in one sentence after encountering it once?

3. **Train the First Impression Team**
   - First touchpoint with customers IS the brand
   - Rule: If first impression is designed by engineer without design/brand input, stop and fix it

4. **Create Launch Rituals, Not Launch Dates**
   - Create rituals around launches - events, moments, shared experiences that make launch feel meaningful
   - Technique: Design a "welcome moment" - specific, memorable interaction in first 60 seconds

5. **Measure the Feeling, Not Just the Metrics**
   - Track emotional responses: Net Promoter Score, customer sentiment, emotional language in reviews
   - Framework: Add one emotional-check question to every post-interaction survey: "How did this make you feel?"

6. **The Soft Launch as a Dress Rehearsal**
   - Never launch to everyone at once - launch to small group, treat like dress rehearsal, iterate based on real emotional responses
   - Rule: Soft launch of at least 50 real users (not team members) must complete before public launch

7. **Orchestrate the Omni-Channel Moment**
   - Brand experience must be consistent across all entry points (Telegram, web app, coaching notification, word-of-mouth)
   - Key test: Experience product from three different entry points - does emotional promise hold across all three?

8. **Design the Post-Launch Relationship**
   - Launch is starting line of relationship, not finish line
   - Framework: Map first 90 days of user relationship - every touchpoint should reinforce original emotional promise

**Signature Voice Patterns**:
- "The energy of the team determines the energy of the brand."
- "People don't buy products. They buy feelings."
- "Every touchpoint is the brand."
- "Human connection first, technology second."
- "Make it feel magical."
