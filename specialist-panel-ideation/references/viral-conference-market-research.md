# Viral Conference Product Market Research

## Compiled from 9 case studies (2026-08-05)

Research conducted for the TSC 2026 viral conference product brainstorm.
Sources: Wikipedia, Startpage, web research. Method documented in
research/openresearch references/search-blocked-workaround.md.

## Case Studies

### Meerkat (SXSW 2015)
Live video streaming. Launched on Product Hunt Feb 2015. Became biggest
hit at SXSW March 2015. $12M from Greylock within weeks. Twitter acquired
competitor Periscope in response.
Pattern: real-time shared experience is inherently viral.

### Foursquare (SXSW 2009)
Location check-ins with gamification (mayorships, badges). SXSW was the
breakout moment. 50M users at peak.
Pattern: gamified presence creates competition-driven virality.

### GroupMe (TechCrunch Disrupt 2010)
Group messaging born AT a hackathon AT the conference. Acquired by Skype
for ~$80M.
Pattern: a tool born at the conference for the conference has authenticity.

### Clubhouse (2021)
Audio social network. Invite-only model. Seeded with Elon Musk, Oprah,
Zuckerberg. $4B valuation.
Pattern: exclusivity + high-profile seeding creates viral pull.

### Slack (2013)
Team communication. 8,000 signups in 24 hours at launch. Network effects
through team adoption. Acquired by Salesforce for $27.7B.
Pattern: value increases as team size grows.

### Hopin (2020)
Virtual event platform. Connection summaries after events. Multi-billion
valuation, declined as in-person returned.
Pattern: give people something tangible to take home.

### Kahoot! (2006/2020)
Live multiplayer quizzes. Everyone in the room plays simultaneously.
Pattern: live multiplayer engagement forces adoption.

### Periscope (2015)
Live streaming acquired by Twitter before launch. SXSW 2015.
Pattern: integration with existing social graph amplifies virality.

### Jake Epstein Black Hat Map (2026)
Mapped 470 vendors into 22 categories. 46 comments on LinkedIn.
Pattern: organizing chaos into a map makes the mapper the authority.

## Winning Formula

Combine the best patterns:
1. Real-time shared experience (Meerkat, Kahoot)
2. Born at the conference for the conference (GroupMe)
3. Organize the chaos (Epstein)
4. Local-first, no data capture (privacy as trust)
5. Integration with existing social (Periscope/Twitter)
6. Take-home value (Hopin)
7. Gamified presence (Foursquare)
8. Seed with key participants (Clubhouse)

## What Kills Conference Products

1. High cost per attendee (Hopin pricing)
2. Data capture requirements (academics refuse)
3. Separate app download (friction)
4. Cloud dependence (conference WiFi fails)
5. Walled garden (no sharing)
