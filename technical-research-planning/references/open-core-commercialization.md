# Open-Core Commercialization Notes

This reference captures a recurring pattern for turning internal methodology into a revenue-maximizing open-core product.

## Core pattern
- Open-source the *standard* so the market adopts the method.
- Keep the *predictive, personalized, and hosted intelligence* proprietary.
- Use the open layer as distribution, credibility, and ecosystem lock-in.
- Use the paid layer for forecasting, premium UX, enterprise dashboards, and managed services.

## Entity/brand separation pattern
- Keep the research lab and IP owner separate from the commercial product company.
- Lab owns methodology, research, and scientific IP.
- Product company owns UX, monetization, customer experience, and delivery.
- If there is a subsidiary, make the ownership and licensing boundary explicit.

## Typical open vs paid boundary
**Open-source**
- scoring SDK or core framework
- templates and quickstarts
- data/spec protocols
- basic reference implementation

**Paid**
- predictive engine
- adaptive personalization
- enterprise reporting
- hosted APIs
- premium coaching or orchestration
- private datasets and tuning logic

## Distribution channels that bootstrap well
- Telegram bot for low-friction conversational access
- web/PWA for richer dashboards
- mini apps or embedded webviews for guided experiences
- voice or SMS fallback for accessibility

## Differentiation patterns that work
- avatar or persona layer that also collects structured data
- game loops: streaks, quests, levels, rewards
- neurodivergent-friendly pacing and adaptive difficulty
- open methodology + premium intelligence combo

## Revenue ladder
1. Immediate digital products or templates
2. Consumer subscription
3. API / developer platform
4. Enterprise pilots and consulting
5. Licensing, data insights, ecosystem marketplace

## Practical guardrails
- Do not leak the premium moat into the public repo.
- Make the open layer genuinely useful, not a teaser stub.
- Use the open layer to earn trust and citations, not to give away forecasting logic.
- Keep naming, ownership, and licensing boundaries explicit in the architecture docs.

## Relevant session learnings
- Divinity Health is the commercial subsidiary/product layer.
- Divinity Science is the 100% founder-owned research lab and IP holder.
- Telegram is a strong bootstrapping channel for health-game UX.
- Personalized avatars are a high-leverage engagement and data-collection layer when implemented with explicit consent and privacy controls.
