# Strategic Foresight Brief Pattern

Session-derived from an AI-paradigm + SpaceX/Mars product brief (July 2026). **Complements** `strategic-intelligence-brief-template.md` (which covers the general format, word-count rules, density techniques, vocabulary constraints table, and research fallback ladder). **This reference** covers the foresight-specific workflow: paradigm mapping, funding-signal tracking, product hypothesis framing, and path-to-attention steps.

## When this pattern applies

- User asks to "map the evolution" of a technology domain and identify what comes NEXT
- User wants a product hypothesis designed for a specific buyer (SpaceX, NASA, enterprise)
- User asks to identify the paradigm AFTER the current leading edge
- Deliverable combines horizon scanning + competitive landscape + named product recommendation

## Foresight research workflow

### Step 1: Map the paradigm stack
Identify the layered evolution. Each wave builds on the previous but solves a limitation the prior wave could not. Example from AI (2026):
- Wave 1: Generative AI (LLMs, diffusion) — pattern synthesis, no grounding
- Wave 2: Agentic AI — LLMs + orchestration scaffolds, reactive not predictive
- Wave 3: World Models — internal environment representations, predictive simulation, solves grounding

### Step 2: Track funding signals (strongest indicator)
The most reliable signal of where a field is heading is where billion-dollar companies are forming. Capture in table format:
- Company name, lead researcher/founder
- Funding amount and date
- Specific technology/architecture name
- What problem it solves that the current paradigm cannot

Real example (July 2026):

| Company | Lead | Positioning |
|---------|------|-------------|
| AMI Labs | Yann LeCun | $1B+ raised, JEPA architecture, LeCun says LLMs "nearing the end" |
| Google DeepMind | Hassabis | Genie 3, real-time 3D world generation at 24fps |
| Nvidia | Jensen Huang | Cosmos 3, open-weight omni-model for physical reasoning |

### Step 3: Split into time horizons
- **2-3 year (emerging now, funded, talent flowing):** The paradigm with real companies and real money.
- **5+ year (theoretical, research-stage, no production breakthrough):** Multiple competing visions — neurosymbolic, continuous learning, open-ended AI, artificial scientific intelligence.

### Step 4: Identify bottlenecks
What prevents each future paradigm from arriving? Table with bottleneck + impact:
- Grounding gap, continual learning, sample efficiency, long-horizon planning, verification/safety

### Step 5: Product hypothesis framing (when a specific buyer is named)

**Naming convention:** Domain-resonant acronym. ARES (Autonomous Resource and Engineering Supervisor) for space/military. Memorable, no conflicts with existing major products.

**Required elements:**
1. What it is (one sentence)
2. Core differentiator vs. generic/off-the-shelf
3. Security/partnership layer (if a partner's expertise is involved)
4. Who buys it (primary + secondary)
5. Why not off-the-shelf internal AI — **this is the most important part. It's the defensibility argument.**

**Key framing principle:** "The orchestration methodology is the product; individual engines are interchangeable." The buyer is buying the architecture, not the components.

### Step 6: Path to attention
Concrete steps with named venues, programs, and timelines.

**Aerospace/Space:** Whitepaper (AIAA Space Forum, IEEE Aerospace) → simulation demo with injected faults → NASA SBIR/STTR Phase I ($125K-$200K) → Phase II ($750K) → dual-use terrestrial markets → SpaceX meeting (18-24 months with real traction).

**Enterprise tech:** Whitepaper → open-source reference implementation → conference talks → pilot deployments → case studies.

**Key principle:** Prove in terrestrial/analog markets first. Marquee customers respect real revenue, not slides.

## Structure template

```
# Strategic Foresight Brief: [Topic]

## SECTION 1: [Domain Evolution]
### Current State Map (wave-by-wave, one line each)
### The Next Wave (near horizon) — company/funding table + grounding argument
### Long Horizon (5+ years) — numbered competing visions with bottleneck status
### Bottlenecks Table
**Bottom line:** synthesis

---

## SECTION 2: [Product/Strategy]
### Target's Stated Needs (specific, verifiable from public sources)
### Why [Methodology] Is Uniquely Suited (numbered mapping)
### Product Hypothesis: [NAME] (what / differentiator / security layer / who buys / why not off-the-shelf)
### Path to Attention (numbered steps with venues, programs, timeline)
```

## Pitfalls

- **Don't hallucinate funding data.** Verify against Wikipedia or press releases. If search engines are blocked, use the fallback ladder in `strategic-intelligence-brief-template.md` and note source limitations in the footer.
- **Don't present a product hypothesis without the "why not off-the-shelf" wedge.** This is the defensibility argument — without it, the hypothesis is just a name.
- **Don't skip path-to-attention.** A product hypothesis without a go-to-market path is incomplete.
- **Don't pad to hit word count.** Dense and under-limit beats padded and at-limit.
