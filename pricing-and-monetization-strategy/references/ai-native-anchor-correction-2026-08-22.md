# AI-Native Anchor Correction (2026-08-22, catch #4)

*Fourth principal rejection in the same campaign, one hour after the labor-hours fix. Extends panel-pricing-defect-2026-08-22.md — read that first.*

## The catch
After re-anchoring the Evidence Pack on Kolabtree's demonstrated $500-960/report (the "correct" fix for labor-hours pricing), the principal rejected it again: "I don't understand who would pay $500 for a generated response from unproven ai."

## Root cause — product-class mis-anchor
Kolabtree's $500-960 buys a **PhD human's accountable month** — the accountability IS the product. We sell generation. Anchoring an AI product on human-service prices imports a premium the product cannot carry: the buyer paying for accountability won't accept AI output, and the buyer who accepts AI output knows its commodity price.

## The three price classes (do not cross-anchor)
| Class | What the buyer pays for | 2026 demonstrated prices |
|---|---|---|
| Human-accountable service | A person staking reputation on the work | Kolabtree $500-960/report, $55-125/hr |
| AI generation as product | Output, no accountability | Elicit/SciSpace $12-49/mo SUBSCRIPTIONS over 138-280M papers; per-report AI research tops at $299-499 (VantaInsights, 48-hr custom) |
| AI output inside custom delivery | A built system + integration + support | Small-biz first AI project $3-10K (taskip/SuperDupr/Latenode converge) |

**Rule:** before benchmarking, name which class your product is in, then anchor ONLY inside that class. An AI product anchored on human-service prices runs 2-5x over its real market and gets caught at first buyer contact.

## The verdict it forced
Standalone AI evidence reports have no demonstrated buyer above the $299-499 band, sitting next to $12/mo subscriptions. The Evidence Pack SKU died entirely — evidence work survives only (a) INSIDE the pilot as day-1 deliverable (class 3 pricing), (b) as free Letters (marketing). A $500-1,000 micro-pilot is the market's own entry step for no-proof sellers.

## Transferable rules
- "Who would pay this, and what EXACTLY are they paying for?" must be answerable in one sentence naming the product class. If the sentence mixes "AI" with a human-service price, the anchor is wrong.
- A subscription commodity adjacent to your SKU caps your standalone price at ~10-40x the subscription's monthly price — above that, only accountability or delivery can carry the number.
- When the principal rejects a number twice, the SECOND re-derivation must change product-class framing, not just the dollar figure. Softening a dead number (list → "buyer-named band") is defending the lane, not fixing it.
