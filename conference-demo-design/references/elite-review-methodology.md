# Elite Review Methodology for Academic Conference Posters

## When to Use
When preparing a poster/presentation for a top-tier academic conference where the audience includes field founders (e.g., Tononi at TSC, Friston at FEP workshops). The goal is not to validate your work (you already know it works) but to identify the questions that will demolish it at the poster session.

## The Four-Reviewer Panel

Assemble reviewers who each see the work from a fundamentally different angle. They must be RUTHLESS. Politeness is a disservice.

### Reviewer 1: The Domain Expert (e.g., Anil Seth for consciousness)
Asks: Is the thesis real or metaphor? What would the field founders say?
- Checks every claim against the specific theory it invokes (IIT 4.0, not IIT 2.0)
- Identifies the single most dangerous claim that could get demolished
- Provides the make-or-break question the poster must answer in 90 seconds

### Reviewer 2: The Network/Methods Scientist (e.g., Barabasi)
Asks: Is the method valid or is it numerology?
- Demands null models, baselines, stability analyses
- Asks "what would this produce if it were random?"
- Identifies whether the method reveals what standard methods cannot

### Reviewer 3: The Meta-Scientist (e.g., Santa Fe Institute)
Asks: Does this matter to the FIELD, not just to the presenter?
- Evaluates catalytic vs incremental potential
- Maps structural holes in the field's topology
- Asks whether the contribution creates a new unit of analysis others can adopt

### Reviewer 4: The Artist/Designer (e.g., Refik Anadol)
Asks: Will people STOP? Will they STAY? Will they REMEMBER?
- Demands the visualization feel ALIVE before interaction
- Specifies exact colors, motion timing, texture, composition
- Identifies the instant-death mistakes that make it look like a startup dashboard

## The Validation Gauntlet (convergence across reviewers)

All four independently converged on the same validation requirements for the constellation concept:

1. **Null model**: What does random mapping produce?
2. **Baseline comparison**: BERT/LDA on same data. Does biological architecture see more?
3. **Stability**: Same input twice, same output?
4. **Ground truth**: Reproduces known theoretical relationships?
5. **External validation**: Predicts co-citation or expert similarity?

If any one of these fails, the constellation is numerology, not science.

## Key Output: The 90-Second Answer

Every poster must have a prepared answer to its central adversarial question. For physiomorphic AI:

**Q: "What does the biological isomorphism BUY you that a flat architecture wouldn't?"**

**A:** The biological organization produces emergent functional properties that a flat architecture cannot replicate. The coupled-oscillator topology reorganizes through genuine phase-synchronization dynamics, not programmed recovery. The thalamic relay gates using oscillatory binding, not priority queues. Health scoring implements interoceptive predictive processing, not threshold monitoring. These produce measurable differences in cascade resilience, integration profiles, and recovery topologies.

## Key Output: Citation Network Overlay

The single most important methodological move for a constellation/map concept: overlay the architectural-resonance map with a citation network. Where they DIVERGE (architecturally resonant but not citation-connected) = true structural holes. Without the overlay, it is clustering. With it, it is an instrument.

## Epistemic Framing Rule

Present as INSTRUMENT OUTPUT ("our system suggests these hypotheses"), never as AI TRUTH ("our AI discovered connections"). The difference between these two framings is the difference between acceptance and rejection at a field with an activated immune system against AI overreach.
