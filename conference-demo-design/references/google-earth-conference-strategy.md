# Google Earth Conference Strategy Research

## The 5 Viral Conference Pillars (from 450 lines of research)

### 1. PRESENTER-DRIVEN, NOT KIOSK
The magic required a skilled operator. The personal Q&A ("Where are you from?") was part of the experience. The operator built suspense before the zoom. A skilled presenter made technology feel magical rather than just functional.

### 2. THE PERSONAL HOOK
"What's your address?" Everyone has one. 15 seconds from space to driveway. Combined narcissism (seeing YOUR home) + visual shock (unprecedented smooth zoom) + cognitive dissonance to awe (whole planet to your car).

### 3. GASPS DRAW CROWDS
Audible reactions of early visitors drew curious onlookers. The "holy shit" moment was visible to passersby. Lines formed naturally.

### 4. EVANGELIST MULTIPLIER
Each person who experienced it immediately told colleagues: "You HAVE to go see this." The recommendation was specific: "They type in YOUR address and fly to YOUR house."

### 5. VIP INFECTION
When keynote speakers experienced the demo privately (flown to their own home, hometown), they referenced it spontaneously in their talks. "Have you seen what they're doing at poster 775?"

## The Original "Holy Shit" Moment
John Hanke typed in Kilday's address (465 Joe Sayers, Austin, TX). In ~15 seconds, the view zoomed from outer space down to Kilday's actual house. He could see his roof, neighbor's trampoline, red Ford Explorer parked out front. "Holy shit!" - Bill Kilday, from "Never Lost Again" (2018).

Source: Bill Kilday, "Never Lost Again: The Rise and Fall and Rise of Google Maps" (2018)

## Conference Timeline
- **SIGGRAPH 2001 (LA):** Dave Lorenzini presented at "3D Roundup" shootout before ~5,000 people. Crowd went wild.
- **SIGGRAPH 2003 (San Diego):** EarthViewer 3D won "Best Product" as voted by attendees.
- **CNN Iraq War (March 2003):** CNN licensed EarthViewer for $75K. The on-screen URL clause (keyhole.com displayed every time) turned every broadcast into free advertising. Servers nearly crashed.

## Comparable Personal-Hook Demos
1. Apple iPhoto/iMovie - "your life, organized" with attendee's own photos
2. Nintendo Wii at E3 2006 - physical personal experience with motion controllers
3. Oculus Rift at E3/GDC 2012-2013 - "you have to try this" lines wrapped around booths
4. Microsoft Photosynth (2006-2008) - zoom through crowdsourced photos to personal photo
5. Spotify Wrapped - personalized data you share

## Key Pattern
The demo works because it is about YOU. Not a generic use case. YOUR house, YOUR photos, YOUR body. This is the most powerful demo strategy in technology.
