# the specialist engine — Adversarial Review Template

## When to use
When a legal panel includes a the specialist engine seat (or the user requests a strategic/power-dynamics review). This is one of the adversarial reviewer options in the 5+5 legal panel pattern.

## Mandate
Analyze the document(s) for POWER dynamics, not legal admissibility or logical soundness. The question is: does the combined narrative make the client look powerful or weak? What leverage is unused? What leverage is accidentally ceded?

## 7-Section Review Structure

### 1. POWERFUL OR WEAK?
Assess whether the combined narrative projects strength or weakness. Look for:
- Reactivity vs. agency (who is the actor, who is the scrivener?)
- Whether the client appears in control or perpetually responding
- Tell-tale signs of weakness: paying for what you claim to own, pre-defending against attacks not yet thrown, knowingly leaving property with someone called dangerous
- The judge reads POWER, not justice — documents that bleed weakness undermine even a strong legal case

### 2. UNUSED LEVERAGE
Identify powerful facts that are buried, underused, or not framed as leverage. Common categories:
- Court actions already won (arrests, protective orders, judicial findings against the opponent)
- Criminal conduct by the opponent that could be reported (stalking, tracking, false reports)
- The opponent's concealed residence or evasiveness with the court
- Veterinary/medical record interference framed as welfare harm, not just "conduct pattern"

### 3. ACCIDENTALLY CEDED LEVERAGE
Identify where the client's own documents give away the store:
- Payment language ("agreed to pay" = negotiation = the other side had something worth paying for)
- Endearments used as title evidence ("Dad" = family = shared custody, not sole ownership)
- Gratuitous acknowledgments (praising the opponent's qualities while arguing unfitness)
- Framework contradictions (invoking a jurisdiction you simultaneously deny exists)
- Speculative exchanges presented as transactions (plane ticket = "exchange for Luna")

### 4. WHAT TO HOLD BACK FOR THE HEARING
Apply the "hold back, not file" principle (see SKILL.md). Identify evidence that is:
- More powerful live than in a filing (visual evidence, escalation patterns spoken aloud)
- Too risky to file (pregnancy claims if any might be true)
- Stronger if surprising (witness identities, hidden text admissions)
- Too weakly sourced for a filing but devastating under cross (prior bad acts)

### 5. THE KEY TRANSACTION — POWER ANALYSIS
If the documents contain a payment, exchange, or transaction that undercuts the core claim, analyze it as the strategic fulcrum. The $7,500 payment pattern:
- Converts OWNER to PURCHASER
- Gives the opponent a receipt saying "these had ransom value"
- No documentation erases a payment
- ONLY recovery: duress framing ("paid under threat of concealment")
- Weak without contemporaneous evidence (police report THAT DAY, protest text, coercion witness)
- Action: Remove transaction language ("agreed to pay," "exchange," "purchase") from ALL documents
- It is a WOUND to cauterize, not a fact to explain

### 6. OPTIMAL STRATEGIC POSITION
Identify the strongest defensible position. If sole ownership is contradicted by the client's own documents, recommend repositioning:
- From "sole owner" → "primary documented caretaker seeking TEMPORARY POSSESSION for STABILITY"
- This does NOT require disproving the opponent's interest
- Makes the transaction payment irrelevant (temporary safety, not purchase)
- Makes documentation dispositive (ALL records are the client's)
- Makes opponent's instability decisive (concealed residence, flight risk, arrest)
- The judge should ask not "Who owns these?" but "Where are they safest RIGHT NOW?"

### 7. WHAT TO CUT
Apply Kahneman cognitive overload findings (40+ claims = judge disengages). Recommend cuts:
- Personal grievance sections unrelated to the core question (pregnancy disputes in a dog filing)
- Weakly sourced character claims (prior euthanasia hearsay — save for cross)
- Defensive sections that signal weakness (anticipated attacks — real power acts)
- Exhaustive summaries (12-point lists → 3 sharp points)
- ALL overlap between documents (one fact, one document; repetition = insecurity)
- Speculative concessions (exchange theories, complimentary acknowledgments)
- ALL characterizations stated as facts ("extortion," "manufacturing," "pattern") — replace with verbs and dates

## Formatting constraint
Machiavelli reviews should be concise and punchy — target under 80 lines. The voice is aphoristic, not academic. Use the header format with author tag ("The Prince | Master of realpolitik").
