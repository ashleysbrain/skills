## Foundational: Cognitive Science of Writing

### Flower & Hayes (1981) — 3,512 citations
- **Title:** "A Cognitive Process Theory of Writing"
- **Venue:** College Composition and Communication, 32(4)
- **DOI:** 10.2307/356600
- **Finding:** Writing is recursive (planning, translating, reviewing
  operate in parallel), not linear stages. Expert writers interrupt
  drafting to replan, re-read to trigger new goals.

### Sommers (1980) — 753 citations
- **Title:** "Revision Strategies of Student Writers and Experienced Adult Writers"
- **Venue:** College Composition and Communication, 31(4)
- **DOI:** 10.2307/356588
- **Finding:** Students revise at word/phrase level (surface). Experts
  revise at argument/discourse level (meaning). Expert revision is
  "finding the argument," not cleanup.

### Kellogg (2008) — 839 citations
- **Title:** "Training writing skills: A cognitive developmental perspective"
- **Venue:** Journal of Writing Research, 1(1)
- **DOI:** 10.17239/jowr-2008.01.01.1
- **Abstract (verbatim):** "Writing skills typically develop over a course
  of more than two decades as a child matures and learns the craft of
  composition through late adolescence and into early adulthood. The novice
  writer progresses from a stage of knowledge-telling to a stage of
  knowledge-transforming characteristic of adult writers. Professional
  writers advance further to an expert stage of knowledge-crafting in which
  representations of the author's planned content, the text itself, and the
  prospective reader's interpretation of the text are routinely manipulated
  in working memory."

### Flower, Hayes, Carey, Schriver & Stratman (1986) — 376 citations
- **Title:** "Detection, Diagnosis, and the Strategies of Revision"
- **Venue:** College Composition and Communication, 37(1), 16-55
- **DOI:** 10.2307/357381
- **Finding:** Three revision subprocesses: Detection (noticing problem),
  Diagnosis (understanding why), Strategy (rewriting). Writers detect
  problems they can't diagnose, leading to superficial fixes.

## AI-Assisted Writing Evidence

### Doshi & Hauser (2024) — 599 citations
- **Title:** "Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the
  collective diversity of novel content"
- **Venue:** Science Advances
- **DOI:** 10.1126/sciadv.adn5290
- **Abstract (verbatim):** "Creativity is core to being human. Generative
  artificial intelligence (AI) — including powerful large language models
  (LLMs) — holds promise for humans to be more creative by offering new
  ideas, or less creative by anchoring on generative AI ideas. We study the
  causal impact of generative AI ideas on the production of short stories
  in an online experiment where some writers obtained story ideas from an
  LLM. We find that access to generative AI ideas causes stories to be
  evaluated as more creative, better written, and more enjoyable,
  especially among less creative writers. However, generative AI-enabled
  stories are more similar to each other than stories by humans alone."

### Bastani, Bastani, Sungu & Ge (2025) — 119 citations
- **Title:** "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence
  from high school mathematics"
- **Venue:** PNAS
- **DOI:** 10.1073/pnas.2422633122
- **Finding:** AI tutoring (hints) improved learning. AI answers (solutions)
  harmed learning. AI doing the cognitive work degrades the writer's own
  construction of understanding.

### Lee, Liang & Yang (2022) — 334 citations
- **Title:** "CoAuthor: Designing a Human-AI Collaborative Writing Dataset"
- **Venue:** CHI 2022
- **DOI:** 10.1145/3491102.3502030
- **Finding:** AI helps when treated as provocation to revise, when human
  maintains authorial control over argument/structure, when used for
  lower-stakes generation.

### Boussioux et al. (2024) — 256 citations
- **Title:** "The Crowdless Future? Generative AI and Creative Problem-Solving"
- **Venue:** Organization Science
- **DOI:** 10.1287/orsc.2023.18430
- **Finding:** Human-AI partnerships outperform human-only when AI is
  collaborator in structured search, not autonomous generator.

### Melumad & Yun (2025) — 27 citations
- **Title:** "Experimental evidence of the effects of large language models
  versus web search on depth of learning"
- **Venue:** PNAS Nexus
- **DOI:** 10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf316
- **Finding:** LLM summaries produce shallower knowledge than web search,
  even with same facts. LLMs inhibit active discovery by presenting
  pre-digested answers.

## Argumentation and Evidence Structure

### Osborne, Erduran & Simon (2004) — 1,540 citations
- **Title:** "Enhancing the quality of argumentation in school science"
- **Venue:** Journal of Research in Science Teaching, 41(10)
- **DOI:** 10.1002/tea.20035
- **Finding:** Scientific argumentation quality depends on explicit
  structure: claims, evidence, warrants, rebuttals. Toulmin model
  adapted for science education.

### Toulmin (1958/2003) — 5,618 citations
- **Title:** "The Uses of Argument, Updated Edition"
- **Venue:** Cambridge University Press
- **DOI:** 10.1017/cbo9780511840005
- **Finding:** Claim, grounds, warrant, backing, qualifier, rebuttal.
  Foundation for all modern argumentation theory.

### Walton, Reed & Macagno (2008) — 1,351 citations
- **Title:** "Argumentation Schemes"
- **Venue:** Cambridge University Press
- **Finding:** Formal classification of argument types (from authority,
  from analogy, cause-to-effect) with critical questions for each.

### Liberati et al. (2009) — 28,000 citations
- **Title:** "The PRISMA Statement for Reporting Systematic Reviews"
- **Venue:** PLoS Medicine
- **DOI:** 10.1371/journal.pmed.1000100
- **Finding:** 27-item checklist for transparent evidence reporting.

### Booth, Colomb & Williams (1995) — 458 citations
- **Title:** "The Craft of Research"
- **Venue:** University of Chicago Press
- **Finding:** Research-to-writing model: topic, question, problem,
  sources, argument, draft, revision. Each stage builds on the previous.
